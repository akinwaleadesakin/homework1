F1 COMPETITION

Built to Compete.

An F1 car is designed not only to be fast, but to perform consistently under the pressure of competition.

RACE PERFORMANCE

Speed Is Only the Beginning

During a Grand Prix, drivers and teams have to manage much more than speed. They must consider tire wear, fuel usage, weather, track conditions, and the performance of competing cars.

Engineers constantly monitor data and communicate with drivers throughout the race. The information they collect can help the team decide when to pit or change its strategy.

During a pit stop, the crew quickly removes the old tires and replaces them with a new set.

03

Strategy

CONSTANT DEVELOPMENT

Building a Faster Car

Formula 1 teams constantly develop their cars throughout a season. Engineers study data from races and testing to identify areas where performance can be improved. Teams may develop new aerodynamic components, suspension systems, cooling solutions, or other upgrades. The constant development of the car is one of the reasons Formula 1 is both a sporting competition and an engineering competition.
