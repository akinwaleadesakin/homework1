F1 TECHNOLOGY

Engineering at 200+ MPH.

Every F1 car is the result of thousands of hours of engineering, testing, and development.

POWER UNIT

Power Meets Efficiency

Modern Formula 1 cars use hybrid power units that combine a turbocharged internal-combustion engine with electric motor systems.

These systems allow the car to produce significant power while recovering and reusing electrical energy. Engineers constantly work to improve performance, reliability, and efficiency.

AERODYNAMICS

Controlling the Air

Aerodynamics is one of the most important parts of Formula 1 engineering. Engineers carefully control airflow around and underneath the car.

The goal is to generate downforce while limiting drag. Downforce pushes the car toward the track, giving the tires more grip and allowing drivers to take corners at extremely high speeds.

DATA & SIMULATION

Every Millisecond Matters

Formula 1 teams collect and analyze enormous amounts of information during races and testing. Engineers monitor speed, tire temperatures, braking, engine performance, fuel usage, and many other systems. Teams also use simulators and computer models to test different setups before taking them to the track.
